Decision lineage · v1–v26

The 26 versions are two investigations, not one.

Both tracks are read directly from the training configurations.

Phase 1 — sixteen versions of rebuilding the data

v1–v16 rebuilt the dataset seven times with the LoRA range fixed at 22–26 — one long data investigation, not sixteen model experiments.

Phase 2 — the sweep only becomes possible once the data stops moving

From v16 the dataset is frozen and only then does the layer range vary — a comparison run on a moving dataset would confound the two.

Normalization

One joint cannot move, and the normalizer does not know that.

This is a fact about the robot until the values pass through a percentile normalizer.

Why the divisor matters here

The divisor is 0.245 against a median of 3.29, so jitter in a barely-moving joint is presented at the same scale as real motion.

What it costs, and what it does not

25.7% of the real-dimension loss, for the joint that moves least. Control accuracy is unaffected; the cost is in the gradient.

Both are measured: the spans against the statistics file, the loss share directly on the checkpoint.

The padding dimensions cost almost nothing

The padding carries 0.92% of the loss and the real dimensions average 383× more per dimension.

It closed the question: under one percent to recover, against discarding pretrained calibration that works. The loss is also not comparable across such a change.

Why it is left alone

Nothing was changed: the seven-dimensional contract matters more, and the accuracy cost is zero. It is recorded because it explains where a large part of the loss goes.

The block the design argument told us to drop

v17 excluded the encoder\u2019s final block on a published argument that the last layer overwrites spatial features and never wins a benchmark.

v23 put it back, and v23 is the version that reached a physical run. The design reasoning was not confirmed by the outcome.

Why the sweep starts in the middle of the encoder

The early blocks transfer and were frozen deliberately; the middle blocks matter because the overhead and side views are not viewpoints the encoder was pretrained on.

Under the same object-naming instruction, the earlier range moved by direction while the wider mid-range version tracked the named object \u2014 a single observation, no trial count.

What this does and does not establish

The decisive comparison is v20 against v23, which differ only by blocks 18 and 26; v21's early-band adaptation gave the slowest recorded execution.

Quantitative measurements exist for v21 and v23 only; the rest are researcher judgements without trial counts. The range is inherited by the xArm6 stage as a prior, not a settled result.
