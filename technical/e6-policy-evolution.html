Decision lineage · v1–v26

The 26 versions are two investigations, not one.

Both tracks are read directly from the training configurations.

Phase 1 — sixteen versions of rebuilding the data

v1–v16 rebuilt the dataset seven times with the LoRA range fixed at 22–26 — one long data investigation, not sixteen model experiments.

Phase 2 — the sweep only becomes possible once the data stops moving

From v16 the dataset is frozen and only then does the layer range vary — a comparison run on a moving dataset would confound the two.

Normalization

One joint barely moves in this task, and the normalizer does not know that.

Not a mechanical limit: the collection procedure varied position mainly in XY with waypoint orientation fixed, and repeatedly selected the same wrist configuration.

Why the divisor matters here

The divisor is 0.245 against a median of 3.29, so jitter in a barely-moving joint is presented at the same scale as real motion.

What it costs, and what it does not

21.9% of the real-dimension loss, for the joint that moves least. Control accuracy is unaffected; the cost is in the objective.

Both are measured: the spans against the statistics file, the loss share directly on the checkpoint.

The padding dimensions cost almost nothing

The padding carries 0.78% of the loss and the real dimensions average 453× more per dimension.

It closed the question: under one percent to recover, against discarding pretrained calibration that works. The loss is also not comparable across such a change.

Whether that share is deserved is a question about the robot, not the statistics

A dimension that barely moves is not automatically one that does not matter, so the question was put to the kinematics instead.

Per degree j5 moves the tool 0.82 mm against 6.98 mm for j1; removing its recorded motion entirely changes the tool path by 0.190 mm over a chunk whose own error is 12.30 mm.

A direct kinematic counterfactual, not a claim the joint could be dropped: what it establishes is that the ordering disagrees.

Replacing the normalizer does not fix it

A standard-deviation normalizer gives 13.44 against 13.43 and raises the share to 29.5%; capping the amplification alone brings it to 5.5%.

Neither correction improved the physical trajectory; the mismatch is real, and the normalizer is the wrong place to address it.

Action metric · offline audit

Asking the robot which action errors matter.

The arm's Jacobian says what a joint error does to the tool, which makes the disagreement measurable before any model is trained.

What was checked before writing any loss

Kinematics agree with the reference to 9.7 × 10⁻⁵ mm; the Jacobian to 9.6 × 10⁻⁹; the linearization holds under 1% error up to 5° per joint; the matrix is PSD to tolerance.

Whether the chunk accumulates was read from the data: 0.000000° maximum reconstruction error over 555 windows against 35.6° for the alternative.

The replacement has to not repeat the original complaint

On its own the new metric spans roughly six thousand to one and gives one joint exactly zero — used alone it would delete two dimensions.

Added to the existing objective rather than replacing it, the smallest eigenvalue is pinned at one. The combined condition number is about 46 — a different kind of quantity from the 13.4× it addresses, so the two are reported side by side.

What this is not yet

The disagreement is real and the kinematics reproduce it. Whether training against the metric produces a better policy is a separate claim awaiting a separate experiment.

The descriptor is specific to an axisymmetric suction cup; a parallel gripper would need a different one. The framework carries over, this descriptor does not.

The comparison, and what was fixed before it started

Two models, same 158 episodes, same seed, differing in one field. Statistics come from the training episodes alone, because the normalization statistic is itself what is being studied.

Training length was fixed from an audit of four earlier checkpoints before either run started; the weighting coefficient was calibrated once and frozen.

What this page is not claiming

The mismatch is measured and the metric is measured. That training against it helps is neither measured nor implied, and will not be stated until the held-out comparison is done.

The block the design argument told us to drop

v17 excluded the encoder\u2019s final block on a published argument that the last layer overwrites spatial features and never wins a benchmark.

v23 put it back, and v23 is the version that reached a physical run. The design reasoning was not confirmed by the outcome.

The band matters; the endpoint does not

Physical recordCode verified

Choosing which band to adapt changed the outcome; moving a band's endpoint by one block did not.

Block 26 is not where the v20–v23 difference comes from; what remains is block 18, the entry to the late band.

The published recommendation is about which layer is read; this sweep only varies where adapters go, and the read point cannot move.

Spend adaptation on the right band; within it, the boundary can be chosen loosely.

Band comparison: quantitative for v21 and v23. Endpoint comparison: a researcher observation without trial counts; a small difference is not a demonstrated absence.

Why the sweep starts in the middle of the encoder

The early blocks transfer and were frozen deliberately; the middle blocks matter because the overhead and side views are not viewpoints the encoder was pretrained on.

Under the same object-naming instruction, the earlier range moved by direction while the wider mid-range version tracked the named object \u2014 a single observation, no trial count.

What this does and does not establish

The heaviest comparison is between bands: the early band gave 115 s and 49 chunks against 62–67 s and 26–28 chunks for the late band.

v18 and v24 differ only by block 26 and behaved comparably, which leaves block 18 as what the v20–v23 comparison is about.

Quantitative measurements exist for v21 and v23 only; the rest are researcher judgements without trial counts. The range is inherited by the xArm6 stage as a prior, not a settled result.

Grounding transfer

The prompts ran. What the arm did with them is not in the record.

The demonstrations contain one object; the language model was never trained here.

Researcher observationCode verified

Downstream-unseen means absent from the local demonstrations, and nothing stronger; the base checkpoint cannot be audited from here.

A charger, a brown egg and the trained box worked; an almond worked alone; a marker produced no target-directed trajectory.

Both controls rest on knowing what was in the scene, and that was not recorded. They are withdrawn.

The almond fell to 2/20 with a competitor present: confidently captured by the similar, larger egg, and unresolved against the dissimilar charger. It is detected alone, so the operator read this as a competition effect rather than a detection failure.

An almond is about one patch wide, so a better-resolved lookalike wins the reference; a marker is long and thin, so no patch holds a compact case for it.

This is preservation, not a new capability: the grounding was inherited and narrow adaptation did not destroy it, and its limits were inherited with it.

No trial count or recording survives; the measure is target-directed motion, not grasping; the marker case is a conditioning failure, and the patch-geometry account is a reading rather than a measured cause.
